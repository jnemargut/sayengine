Life, Crowds and Traffic

Technical 🔁 Two-way door High stakes

Terrarium's folk, wildlife, cars and boats took about 12,000 lines across 11 separate modules, and the bugs repeated: jitter, pops of up to 2 m, folk sitting inside each other on the boat, a dog inside its walker, boats paddling through walls, and the player colliding with nothing until decision 183. The life module (decision 26) decides how towns, real or invented (decision 27), get people, animals and vehicles that go about their day in both Godot and the web, and how "nothing passes through anything" is checked before you ever see it.

People stay simple; places carry the behaviour (the Sims rule: "dumb people, smart objects"). A bench offers two seats, a dog offers "walk me" and becomes linked to its walker. Each town gets a cast and a schedule from a seed, by hour, season and event, so the same day always plays the same way. The studio bakes one walk mesh and one lane graph (roads from map data for real towns, from rules for invented ones) and exports the same polygons to both engines. A small runtime in each engine follows lanes, does simple car following with stop lines, and adds gentle avoidance. A sped-up headless day checks for overlaps, wall crossings and pops before you see it.

Works well when

  • New behaviour should come from new places, not new code
  • Bugs must be caught before you look

Watch out for

  • Two small runtimes to keep in step (TypeScript + GDScript)
  • Not truly emergent; life follows schedules

The case against my pick

A means writing and keeping two small runtimes in step, one in TypeScript and one in GDScript, and drift between them is the same class of bug as Terrarium's "same measurements as the viewer". Seeded schedules make towns predictable; a player who comes back at 3pm sees the same people doing the same thing. Nobody publishes how many agents a phone can carry, so the budget is a guess until measured. If your next game only needs a few characters, B gets there in days, not weeks.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes lane graphs can come from map data (osm2streets-style) for real towns and from the same rules that lay out invented towns.
  • Assumes the runtime stays small (a few hundred lines per engine) with the same tests run against both.
  • Tripwire: if the two runtimes disagree on the same seeded day by more than 0.5 m, move the logic into one shared core (WASM for web, GDExtension for Godot).
  • Tripwire: measure agents per frame on a real iPhone (decision 31) before promising a crowd size.

Research context

  • The Sims put behaviour in objects that "advertise" what they offer, so new behaviour comes from new objects, not new agent code.Wikipedia
  • Unreal's City Sample moves pedestrians and traffic along authored lanes, not a navmesh, with avoidance only as a nudge and separate simulation and visual detail levels.Epic docs
  • Godot's avoidance "exists in its own space and has no information from navigation meshes or physics collision", and many agents cost a lot.Godot docs
  • Detour's crowd (in recast-navigation-js) keeps agents on the navmesh, with a 6-neighbour limit; a pure TypeScript version (navcat) exists but is slower.Recast docsnavcat benchmark
  • osm2streets turns map tags into lane-level roads and intersections and runs in the browser; intersections are still hard where map data is thin.osm2streets
  • SUMO is too heavy for games; a lane graph with simple car-following (IDM) and stop lines fits in a few hundred lines.RoadTrafficSimulator
  • Pre-recorded "crowd patches" cut run-time cost sharply, trading quality, memory and speed.EPFL
  • ORCA-style avoidance deadlocks in symmetric flows (the doorway case) without a passing-side rule.arXiv 2407.16771
  • Godot bakes navmeshes with its own cell sizes; a separately baked web mesh will differ, so bake once and export the same polygons.Godot NavigationMesh

Where experts disagree

Unreal's City Sample says "lanes for everyone, avoidance as a nudge"; Godot's tools say "navmesh plus avoidance". A takes the lanes side. No source gave a trustworthy number of agents a phone can carry at 60 fps, so that has to be measured.
