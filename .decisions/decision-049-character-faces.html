Faces and Speech Beyond the Template Face

Technical 🔁 Two-way door High stakes

Today's lip sync, blinks and expressions (15 speech shapes, about 18 expressions, teeth and tongue) were built for one face. Skeleton-first characters (decision 47) get faces assembled from parts: a wide mouth, a beak, a muzzle, a mask, a long nose. Generating speech shapes for an arbitrary face is research-grade work, but these faces aren't arbitrary: the studio built them from known parts. This decides how a face made of parts gets to speak and emote.

Option A
My pick: each face part knows how to talk Strong pick Chosen

Face Parts That Carry Their Own Speech Shapes

Saying "oh": round mouth · robot speaker bars · beak opening · goblin wide mouth
Mouth part defines its own 15 speech shapes: a beak opens and clacks, a speaker grille lights in bars
Eye and brow parts define blinks, glances and the expression set
Teeth and tongue come with mouths that have them
Checks per face: lips close on M/B/P, speech shapes look different from each other
What it feels like: give the goblin a wide mouth part and it lip-syncs like the blacksmith. Give the forest creature a beak part and the same line makes the beak open on vowels and snap shut on M, B and P. Every face is assembled from parts that already know how to talk.
Every face part comes with its own motion. A mouth part defines how its 15 speech shapes look (lips round and close; a beak opens, narrows and snaps shut; a robot's speaker bars light up), and eye and brow parts define blinks, glances and expressions. Assembling a face assembles its speech. The existing lip sync, blinks and checks work unchanged because every mouth answers to the same 15 names. New mouth styles are new parts, made once and reused.

Works well when

  • Any face the studio builds can speak
  • Lip sync and checks stay as they are
  • New styles added one part at a time

Watch out for

  • Each new mouth style needs its shapes made once
  • Parts must blend cleanly into different heads

Option B

Generate Speech Shapes on Any Finished Face

Template shapes moved onto a new face by matching landmarks
What it feels like: any face, however it was made, gets speech shapes automatically. Great on humanlike faces, unreliable on beaks, snouts and masks, where there's no "lip" to find.
Find landmarks on any finished face (lip corners, jaw, eyes) and transfer the template's speech shapes onto it. Works on faces the studio didn't build from parts, too. But it's research-grade: face detectors fail on stylised, low-poly and non-human faces, and a beak or mask has no lips to map, so results vary face by face.

Works well when

  • Humanlike faces of any origin

Watch out for

  • Unreliable on stylised faces

Option C

Painted 2D Mouths and Eyes for Everyone

Mouth frames swapped like a cartoon (6–9 frames)
What it feels like: a charming cartoon style, and it works on any head. But it's flat: no teeth, no jaw, and it overrides the 3D faces you already have.
Every character uses painted mouth and eye frames swapped like a cartoon (Rhubarb's 6–9 mouth shapes). Works on any head shape and reads clearly. But it changes the whole style to flat toon faces, gives up the teeth, tongue and 3D shapes built in week 4, and suits some games but not all.

Works well when

  • Toon, paper or pixel styles

Watch out for

  • One flat style for all

Option D

Jaw and Eyes Only for Non-Template Faces

The jaw flaps with the voice; eyes blink and glance
What it feels like: it reads as "talking", like a puppet, and works on anything. But it isn't lip sync: no M/B/P closures, no rounded "oo".
New faces get a hinged jaw that opens with the voice, plus blinks and glances; only the human template keeps full lip sync. Works on every head and costs little. But it reads as a puppet flapping, not speech, which undoes the week-4 work for every non-human character.

Works well when

  • Background characters

Watch out for

  • Not real lip sync

The case against my pick

A only works for faces made of the studio's parts, so it does nothing for the occasional outside character (decision 50); B's landmark transfer would help both. Each new mouth style (beak, muzzle, grille) is a small project of its own, so variety in mouths grows only as fast as parts get made. And parts must blend cleanly into very different heads, or faces will look like masks stuck on.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Starting parts: the current human mouth (with teeth and tongue), a small cartoon mouth, a beak, a muzzle, a speaker-grille mouth; three eye and brow styles.
  • Every mouth answers to the same 15 speech-shape names and the same expression names, so lip sync, performances and checks don't change.
  • Tripwire: if a part looks stuck on in renders, add a blend step that smooths where it meets the head.
  • For outside characters, decision 50 picks the fallback (their own shapes, jaw only, or B).
