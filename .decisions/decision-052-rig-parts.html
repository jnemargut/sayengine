How Any Shape Gets a Skeleton

Technical 🚪 One-way door High stakes

You want anything you ask for to move: snakes, octopuses, spiders, fish, flying birds, mechs, cars, doors, windmills, plants in the wind. Today the studio animates six body plans plus tails and wings, each with motion computed from its proportions (decisions 47–48). This decides the general way any shape gets bones and knows how to move. Research found that every option that's local, free and safe to ship commercially is procedural: AI motion models are humanoid-only, non-commercial, or need a 24 GB NVIDIA GPU.

What it feels like: you say "a crab robot with a whip tail" and the studio assembles six legs and a tail chain. It walks sideways right away, legs stepping when they can no longer reach, body riding on its feet, tail lashing. "Make the tail lazier" is one setting.
The studio builds every skeleton from a small set of parts, and each part comes with its own procedural motion (the approach Spore used to animate creatures nobody had seen before). Legs of any count step and plant by IK, chains ripple, coil and reach, wings flap and glide, fins and bodies swim with a travelling wave, wheels roll on springy suspension, hinges and sliders swing within limits, springs add follow-through, and plants sway in the shader. Today's body plans become ready-made combinations. Exports bake clips for both engines, and interactive parts (feet on uneven ground, wheels) also run live using Godot 4.6's new IK nodes and a matching web runtime.

Works well when

  • "Anything I ask for" moves right away
  • New creatures from parts you already have
  • Local, free, safe to ship

Watch out for

  • Eight part types to build and tune
  • Procedural motion needs style settings to feel alive

The case against my pick

Spore's own team said it took weeks to learn which motions generalise across shapes, and generalised procedural motion tends to look correct but generic. Eight part types is a big build, and Godot's new IK nodes are only weeks old, with bugs reported. B's hand-built body types would look better for the few creatures you actually use, and if your games mostly need people and a dog, the general system may be more than you need.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes today's six body plans re-express as part combinations without changing how they look or move.
  • Assumes interactive parts run live in both engines (Godot 4.6 IK nodes; a matching web runtime), and everything else bakes to clips.
  • Tripwire: if a part type looks generic after its style settings, allow hand-tuned overrides per creature (B on top of A).
  • Tripwire: if Godot's IK nodes misbehave, bake those motions to clips for Godot instead of running them live.
