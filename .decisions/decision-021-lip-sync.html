Where Lip Sync Comes From

Architecture 🔁 Two-way door High stakes

Faces now have the 15 standard speech shapes (decision 20). Something has to say which shape, when, and how strongly, for every line of dialogue. The research is consistent on what makes a mouth look real: not more shapes, but timing and blending. Neighbouring sounds shape each other ("coarticulation"), and lips must fully close on M, B and P. Snapping from shape to shape is what made Mass Effect Andromeda's faces infamous. Every option below feeds the same final step: a smoothing pass that blends shapes and enforces closures.

Audio + Script, Aligned Locally

1Any audio: your recording, an actor, or any text-to-speech voice
↓
2Align with the script: word and sound timings (Rhubarb, or Whisper timestamps + a pronunciation dictionary)
↓
3Sounds → the 15 speech shapes
↓
4Smoothing: blend neighbours, close lips on M/B/P, scale by emotion
↓
5Shape curves baked into the cutscene; reviewable
Use any audio: you reading the lines, an actor, or any text-to-speech voice. In step 2 the studio aligns the audio with the script on your Mac, using Rhubarb Lip Sync (open source, made for games) or Whisper word timings plus a pronunciation dictionary for more precise sounds. Step 4 is where the realism comes from: shapes overlap and blend, lips always close on M/B/P, and the emotion of the line scales the mouth. It's free, offline, and doesn't care where the voice came from.

Works well when

  • Any voice source, including your own
  • Offline on your Mac, no service fees
  • Cutscenes (offline is fine)

Watch out for

  • English is most accurate; other languages rougher
  • The smoothing pass needs real tuning

Let a cloud text-to-speech service generate the voice and hand back timing in the same call. Azure returns mouth-shape curves directly; ElevenLabs returns per-letter timings. It's the most automatic option, since Claude writes a line and gets voice plus mouth together. But it only works for voices from that service: your own recordings or an actor wouldn't get lip sync. Users report Azure's mouth curves run too wide.

Works well when

AI Audio-to-Face (Audio2Face)

1Any audio
↓
2NVIDIA model needs an NVIDIA GPU (Windows/Linux), not your Mac
↓
3Outputs whole-face motion incl. emotion, as ARKit weights
↓
4Fold 52 → your ~30 shapes
↓
5Review
NVIDIA open-sourced Audio2Face in 2025 (commercial use allowed). From audio alone it produces the whole face: mouth, plus brows, cheeks and emotion it infers. It's the highest quality available. But it needs an NVIDIA graphics card on Windows or Linux, so on your Mac it means a cloud machine. It outputs ARKit's 52 shapes, which need folding into your set, and it's tuned for realistic faces, not stylized ones.

Works well when

  • You have an NVIDIA machine or cloud budget
  • Want emotion inferred from the voice too

Watch out for

  • Won't run on your Mac
  • Tuned for realistic faces

The case against my pick

Alignment tools only know when sounds happen, not how a person feels saying them. A's mouths will be well-timed but emotionally flat unless the smoothing pass and the performance layer (decision 23) add expression on top, which is real tuning work. Audio2Face infers emotion from the voice itself and simply looks better, if you can run it. And if every line in your games will be AI-voiced anyway, B is fewer moving parts. My answer: A as the default, with B's timings and C's output accepted as alternative inputs into the same smoothing step, since all three end in the same 15 shapes.

This recommendation assumes

  • Cutscene dialogue is pre-recorded or pre-generated, so offline processing is fine.
  • English first; other languages work with lower accuracy.
  • The smoothing pass (blend, M/B/P closure, emotion scaling) is ours and is where quality is won. It gets automated checks: lips closed on every P/B/M, no shape held unnaturally long.
  • Option D's text-only mode is kept as a drafting tool for cutscenes before voices exist.

What the research says

  • Rhubarb Lip Sync is an offline command-line tool built for games. It takes audio plus an optional script (recommended for accuracy) and outputs timed mouth shapes as JSON. Its default recognizer is English-only. A Godot add-on for it already exists. Rhubarb Godot Baked Lipsync
  • Snapping one sound to one shape looks "robotic or creepy". The fix is coarticulation, where neighbouring sounds shape each pose, with each shape on an attack, peak and decay curve. JALI, SIGGRAPH 2016
  • Volume-only lip sync can't place M/B/P closures, which causes mouth "flapping". A transcript fixes much of this. Rhubarb author
  • Azure text-to-speech returns 55 face values per frame at 60 fps, the first 52 in ARKit order. ElevenLabs returns per-letter timings, with no mouth shapes. Microsoft Learn ElevenLabs
  • Audio2Face-3D was open-sourced in September 2025: MIT-licensed tools, commercial-use model licence. It needs an NVIDIA GPU with CUDA on Windows or Linux. NVIDIA Model card
  • Mass Effect Andromeda's widely mocked faces likely came from dialogue animated algorithmically at scale with little polish. Automation without a quality pass is the failure mode. Vice
