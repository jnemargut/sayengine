Real Places to Scenes

Technical 🚪 One-way door High stakes

The world module (decision 26) turns a place, real or invented, into a low-poly game scene: ground, buildings, roads, water and trees. Terrarium did this with 20+ fetch scripts, seven fixer scripts and 26 hand fixes that a rebuild could wipe out. This decision picks where the shapes come from and how your fixes survive. No splats (decision 28).

Option A's pipeline, with "where the town plan comes from" made a plug-in step. Real data is one source; an invented plan (from a style and a street sketch, or from a short description Claude turns into streets and lots) is another; a remix (real streets, invented buildings) is a third. After that, everything is shared: the rules that make clean low-poly buildings, the per-region styles, the checks, and the fix layer. Invented towns get stable IDs from their seed and lot numbers, so your fixes survive a regenerate the same way they survive a data refresh. C's "invented from the street map" becomes one of A's sources instead of a separate path.

  • One set of building rules and checks to get right

Watch out for

  • The town plan format has to suit both real and invented towns from day one
  • Invented layouts need their own rules for believable streets

Start from 3D city models that already exist and simplify them into the low-poly style. Closest to the real shapes where a city publishes good models, which is how Terrarium got its downtown. But Google's photo-realistic tiles are off the table (their terms forbid derived objects, caching and offline use), city models cover only some places, simplifying scanned meshes gives Terrarium's "melted stepped heap", and fixes have no stable thing to attach to.

Works well when

  • One city with a good open 3D model
  • Exact shapes matter most

Watch out for

  • Google tiles can't be used
  • Melted, blobby buildings
  • Fixes lost on refresh

The case against my pick

A is a real pipeline, about as big as everything Terrarium's ingest/ and convert/ folders became, and "rules" still produce the generic box-with-a-roof look for ordinary buildings. US LiDAR is mostly 2 points per m², a quarter of what the best open roof fitter needs, so most roofs will come from tags and guesses, not scans. If what you really want is a few charming, recognisable blocks rather than whole towns, D (hand-traced) plus C (invented filler) gets there sooner and looks more loved.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes Overture's building IDs stay stable across releases, as promised, with a position match as a fallback.
  • Assumes credits on a splash or credits page, plus publishing your fixed building data, is acceptable for your games (ODbL).
  • Tripwire: if more than about 10% of fixes "lose their building" on a refresh, switch keys to position plus shape.
  • Tripwire: if rule-built ordinary buildings look generic in the look lab (decision 29), add per-region style rules before adding more data.
