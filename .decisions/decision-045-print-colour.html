Colour Without an AMS

Visual 🔁 Two-way door Medium stakes

Your A1 mini has one spool and no AMS, so a print is one colour unless you change filament by hand. Studio models already have materials and colours (a walnut seat, black legs). This decides what /studio print does with those colours: ignore them, turn them into a pause where you swap filament at a height, split the model into parts you print in different colours, or leave colour for paint.

Option A
My pick: one colour by default, two tricks when you want more Strong pick

One Colour, Plus Height Swaps and Colour-by-Part

Swap at 3.2 mm: white plate, black letters on top
Mini stool: seat printed in brown, legs in black, snapped together
Default: one colour; studio colours become a hint, not a problem
Height swap: the studio places a filament-change pause at the layer where colour changes
By part: one project per colour, parts joined with snap-fits or pegs (decision 46)
What it feels like: "print this sign, black letters on white". The studio sees the colour change is purely by height, adds a pause at 3.2 mm, and Bambu Studio stops there for you to swap spools. For a two-colour stool, it makes two plates, one per colour, with snap-fit joints.
Print one colour by default and treat studio colours as a hint. When colours split cleanly by height (signs, badges, coasters, text on top), the studio adds a filament-change pause at that layer, which Bambu Studio supports on a single-spool printer: it stops, you swap, it carries on. When colours belong to separate parts (a seat and legs), the studio makes one plate per colour and joins the parts with the fit helpers from decision 46. Anything else gets one colour plus a note.

Works well when

  • Mostly single-colour prints with the odd colourful one
  • No extra hardware
  • No waste from purging

Watch out for

  • Height swaps need you at the printer
  • Parts need joining

One Colour, Always

Sign: letters raised, same colour
Everything in whatever spool is loaded
What it feels like: nothing to think about. Colours in the model are ignored, and you rely on raised detail and shape for contrast.
Ignore colours and print everything in the loaded spool. Nothing to build and nothing to babysit. But signs, badges and two-tone objects lose their contrast, and a single-spool printer can do more with a pause.

Works well when

  • Functional parts

Watch out for

  • No colour at all

Every colour its own print, glued or snapped together

Every colour becomes its own set of parts, printed separately and assembled. Works for any colour layout. But simple cases like a sign become fiddly inlay assembly, and every print in more than one colour means multiple plates and glue or joints.

The case against my pick

For occasional prints, B's "one colour, always" may be all you ever use, and the height-swap and split-by-part logic would be code that rarely runs. Height swaps also mean standing by the printer at the right moment, which is easy to miss on a long print. If you buy an AMS lite later, the by-part plumbing changes anyway (one plate, filament per part), so building it now may be partly wasted.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes Bambu Studio's "pause / change filament at layer" works on the A1 mini without an AMS, written into the project file.
  • Assumes the studio can tell when colours split cleanly by height (all faces of a colour above or below one layer).
  • Tripwire: if you buy an AMS lite, switch colour-by-part to one plate with filament per part.
