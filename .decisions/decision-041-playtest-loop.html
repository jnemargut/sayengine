What it feels like: /x-playtest asks what this test is for, you play and then type what you thought, bots play 40 times overnight, and you get one page where each finding links to the decision it questions. "Revisit decision 3?" reopens that page with the playtest attached.
Every playtest is an experiment with a question, the way Valve runs them. You play (or a friend does), then type your thoughts afterwards in a plain text box; talking out loud with local transcription is there if you want it, never required. Claude sorts the notes into fun moments and confusions. Bots play the build many times and measure what people can't see: stall spots, time per delivery, falls, frame time. "Great v1" is a short checklist from the research: the 30 seconds of fun holds up, a newcomer gets it without help, and every pillar shows in play. Each finding links to the decision it questions, with a one-click "revisit".

Works well when

  • You want feedback to change decisions, not sit in notes
  • Fun from you, numbers from bots
  • Local and free

Watch out for

  • Bots need a goal they can play toward
  • Newcomer tests need real people

The case against my pick

A asks a lot of a solo developer: a question before every test, talking out loud, a form, bot goals to write. For an early toy that's ceremony; B's "play and jot" may get the same insight in five minutes. Bots need a goal they can play toward, which a cozy toy may not have, and writing one can take longer than playing yourself. And the "newcomer gets it" check needs real people anyway, which is D's whole point.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes the playtest question is suggested by Claude from the current stage (toy: "is it fun to play with?"; loop: "does it hold up?"), so you only confirm it.
  • Assumes bots reuse the life module's runtime and the proof module's capture, so they cost little to add.
  • Your notes are typed after playing by default; talking out loud with local transcription is optional.
  • Tripwire: if even typed notes get skipped, ask one question after each playtest instead.
  • Before calling v1 done, run at least one D-style round with real newcomers.

Research context

  • Valve: the goal of playtesting is fun, not bug-finding; each test is an experiment on a design hypothesis, using observation, think-aloud, Q&A, surveys and metrics.Valve, GDC 2009
  • Riot playtests "ugly but fun" prototypes early and often, including outside the team.Riot R&D
  • Onboarding has to be tested with real newcomers, because developers can't see their own game fresh.Celia Hodent
  • "30 seconds of fun over and over" as the core test of a great game.Griesemer
  • Design pillars are the filter every feature, and every playtest finding, passes through.Game Developer
