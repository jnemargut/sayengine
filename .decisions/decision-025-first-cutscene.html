The case against my pick

A stretches the plan from six weeks to eight and pushes the walking diorama back a week, so you wait longer for anything playable. The kit in week 3 is also asked to do a lot at once: body, face template, full hands, all with clean weights. That's the most error-prone week of the plan. If the week-4 talking-face checkpoint fails, everything after it waits on fixing it. That's the point of the checkpoint, but it can stall the whole plan. If playable gameplay matters more to you than cutscenes right now, C keeps the diorama on schedule, at the cost of reworking the kit.

This recommendation assumes

  • Roughly full-time with Claude Code, as before; weeks are estimates.
  • You record a few test lines (or pick a text-to-speech voice) by week 4 for the checkpoint.
  • Week-4 checkpoint tripwire: if the talking face doesn't read as speech after a week of tuning, stop and revisit decisions 19–21 (denser face, or Audio2Face on hero lines) before building further.
  • Week-8 tripwire: if the cutscene isn't playing in Godot, cut the three.js player to a later milestone.
  • D (a branching story scene) is the next milestone after A.

What the research says

  • One indie studio, unable to afford animators or motion capture, captured about 350 facial animations with one person and a budget camera. Cleanup took the time. Outsourcers say cost pushes studios to polish only selected shots. Well Told Entertainment MCV
  • Mass Effect Andromeda shows that bad faces dominate how a whole game is received. Proving the face early, before building on it, is cheap insurance. PC Gamer
  • The thin-slice first build found about a dozen real bugs in a week because the whole loop ran end to end. That's the case for one small scene through all the new parts. bench/TASKS.md
