What it feels like: /x-decide-gaming a cozy game where you deliver bread around a tiny invented town at dawn sends you to the concept skill. Four or five decisions later, the design skill picks up from your choices. /x-game-build then turns them into a first playable, stopping where a call is yours.
Mirror decision-kit, made for games. One router (the only skill Claude calls on its own) sends you to the right stage. Two thinking skills cover concept (fantasy, toy or loop, pillars, scope) and design (camera, controls, character, feel, look, world, cast). Two action skills cover building (compile decisions into a build spec the studio tools run) and playtesting (play it, turn findings into revisited decisions). Every skill uses decision-kit's .decisions/ format, so /x-challenge, /journal and /brief work on games for free.

Works well when

  • You want to stop and resume at any stage
  • Each skill stays small (under 500 lines)
  • It should feel like decision-kit

Watch out for

  • Four new skills to keep consistent
  • The concept and design boundary needs care

An Autonomous Builder That Pauses at Big Calls

/x-decide-gaming "a bread delivery game"
build → generate assets → run → prove, on a loop

What it feels like: godogen-style. A playable appears fast, and you weigh in at three or four moments. But most decisions get made for you while you aren't looking.
One loop builds the game on its own (build, make assets, run, check), pausing for you only at three or four big moments. Fastest to something playable, and it's how godogen, the closest existing tool, works. But it's the opposite of decision-kit: the many small calls that shape a game (camera, feel, scope) get made silently, which is the "vibe coding" decision-kit exists to avoid.

Works well when

  • Throwaway experiments
  • Speed over say

Watch out for

  • Decisions made for you
  • Against decision-kit's whole idea

The case against my pick

Four new skills plus a router is real upkeep, and the line between "concept" and "design" is blurry for games (is the camera a concept call or a design call?), so the two skills could overlap and Claude could pick the wrong one. C reuses everything you've already tuned in the x-skills and would ship in a day. And godogen shows people like seeing a playable fast; A could feel slow if the first build comes only after eight or nine decisions.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes only the router is auto-invoked; the four children run when the router or you call them, so their descriptions can't collide.
  • Assumes the concept skill reaches a first playable toy early (decision 37 settles when), so A doesn't feel slow.
  • Tripwire: if you keep jumping between concept and design mid-game, merge them into one design skill.
  • Tripwire: if any skill passes 500 lines, move detail into one-level references/ files.
