Checking Any Motion Is Believable

Technical 🔁 Two-way door High stakes

The crouched walk, the board-like apron and the floating hammer were all caught by checks or by looking, and every one of them would multiply across spiders, octopuses and windmills. With rig parts (decision 52) and key-posed actions (decision 53), motion can be anything, so checks can't be written per creature. This decides how any motion gets checked, and how you review it.

What it feels like: every motion gets the same checks, because each rig part says what "right" means for it: legs say where feet must stay planted, wheels say they must roll at ground speed, hinges say their limits. A timeline shows exactly where it fails ("tentacle through the teapot, 1.7–1.9 s"), and you watch the clip with stills at each key moment.
Each rig part brings its own definition of correct: legs and paws must plant without sliding (measured relative to the body's size); wheels roll at ground speed; hinges stay in their limits; chains don't fold through themselves; springs settle. On top, universal checks run on every motion: nothing passes through anything, legged bodies keep their centre of weight over their feet, no sudden pops, loops close, clips end in the idle pose. Failures show on a timeline with times, and you review the clip with stills at each key moment. Checks are the authority; your eye judges charm.

Works well when

  • Any creature, no new checks to write
  • Problems found before you watch
  • You see exactly where it breaks

Watch out for

  • Can't judge whether it looks good
  • Thresholds need tuning per part

The case against my pick

General checks can pass motion that still looks wrong: the crouch would have passed "feet planted" and "no penetration" and was only caught by an upright check written specifically for it. Research has no agreed standard for motion plausibility, and thresholds vary paper to paper, so each part's limits will need tuning. Checks keep obvious bugs away from you but can't replace your eye for whether a motion has character.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Each rig part declares its contacts, limits and form rules (for example, legs: planted feet, body height relative to leg length).
  • Foot sliding measured as a share of frames where a planted foot slides beyond a small, body-relative distance.
  • Today's specific checks (upright walk, apron bend, strike form) stay as extra form checks on top.
  • Tripwire: if you spot a motion problem the checks passed, add the rule to that part so it's caught next time.
