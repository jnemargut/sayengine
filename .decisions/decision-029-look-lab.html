A look is one record: tonemapper (pinned, since it changes saturation), a colour grade, toon and outline settings, palette, fog, sky and light, and material tweaks. One record drives the studio view, Godot, three.js, bakes, thumbnails and trailers. Each look has a board of your reference pictures and measured targets: a colourfulness range (so "not too saturated" becomes a number), a palette match and a style-match score from a small local model. Claude may only judge looks as A/B picks. Where a renderer can't do colour grading (Godot's web/low-end mode), the look bakes into the textures.

Works well when

  • You steer with pictures and words like "not too saturated"
  • The same look must match in Godot, web and phone
  • Looks change often and need to be compared

Watch out for

  • Numbers catch "too saturated", not "no charm"
  • Matching two engines' tonemappers takes care

Grade colours and palette into the textures at bake time and use no full-screen effects. It looks identical in every engine and on every phone, and costs nothing at run time. But it rules out most of what made Terrarium's looks: glow, outlines, wet reflections, pixel, pencil and poster effects. Good as the fallback inside A, too limited as the whole answer.

Works well when

  • Phones and low-end devices come first
  • Simple, flat looks

Watch out for

  • No glow, outlines or screen effects
  • Changing a look means re-baking everything

The case against my pick

Your redos were about charm and mood ("way less sterile", "a look like this"), and no colourfulness number or style score measures charm. A could pass every gate and still feel wrong, and the record format may box in the odd, wonderful one-off look that free shader code (B) allows. Getting Godot and three.js to match colour for colour is fiddly: they share only some tonemappers, and nobody has published a parity test, so the first look may take longer than writing it by hand.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes a look can carry custom shader code as an escape hatch, for the one-off look that doesn't fit the record.
  • Assumes a small local style model (CSD or DINOv2-small) runs in under a second per frame on the M2 Air.
  • Tripwire: if Godot and web renders of the same look differ visibly after pinning the tonemapper, grade in the bake for web and accept small differences.
  • Tripwire: if the numbers pass but you redo a look twice anyway, drop the style score and keep only colourfulness and the A/B board.

Research context

  • Godot's colour grade takes a 3D lookup texture and runs after tonemapping, but only in Forward+ and Mobile; the Compatibility (web/low-end) renderer has no colour adjustments at all.Godot EnvironmentGodot rendering limits
  • three.js loads .cube colour grades directly (LUTCubeLoader + LUTPass); Godot needs a converted strip image.three.js PRGD Grader
  • The tonemapper is part of the look: AgX desaturates colours heavily, Khronos Neutral keeps them faithful.Blender proposal
  • The VRM toon material (MToon) has maintained versions in both three.js and Godot, the only shared cross-engine look standard found.three-vrm
  • Hasler–Süsstrunk colourfulness tracks human ratings at over 90%, with a scale from about 15 (slightly) to 82 (highly colourful).HSM3
  • A style-specific embedding (CSD, 2024) beats general image models at matching style, not content.CSD paper
  • Vision models are poor taste judges: 26.5% vs 68.9% for experts; ask for A/B picks, not scores.Visual Aesthetic Benchmark
  • Studios turn a look into a few named, testable rules (Saints Row: The Third used seven).GDC Vault
  • Small (16³) colour grades band on skies and fog; use 33³ with dithering.SMPTE journal
