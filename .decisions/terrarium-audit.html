What kept coming back

The look, over and over

  • The look a first-time visitor sees changed four times: Toy, then Dreamlike, then Photo, then Diorama.
  • Clay, Cel, Neon and Poster each needed a redo or a second pass; Lifelike and Blocky were added and then removed.
  • You steered with reference pictures and "not too saturated", twice.

Phones

  • 123 commits, 49 of them fixes: a crash when changing look, an iPhone Safari freeze, walls that fizzed only on the phone.
  • Page limits were found by crashing (maxPagedSplats 64 vs 420).
  • Speed was only ever checked on an imitated phone; you confirmed the real one by hand.

Buildings from survey data

  • A melted, stepped Legislative Building; sawtooth walls; a hole through the State Capitol.
  • A chain of seven fixer scripts, from outlines and roofshape to oddballs.
  • 26 hand fixes that a data rebuild could wipe out; rebuild_town.py is called "the riskiest thing we do".

Things inside things

  • About 55 commits with "through" or "inside": folk sitting inside each other on the boat, a dog inside its walker, paddling through walls, floating window panes.
  • The player didn't collide with anything until decision 183.

Folk and creatures

  • Jitter, pops of up to 2 m, the no-arms rule breaking again, outfits shredding when remeshed.
  • Seven separate *life.js and four *wild.js modules (~12k lines): one idea, written seven times.

Video, names, and the Unity detour

  • The explainer video was recut five times; filming lightning and home runs needed hooks (__strike, __homer).
  • About 164 commits on names and the About page; brands were kept, then removed.
  • 17 decisions spent on a Unity port that ended up as a "lab".

What you kept pushing back on

Don't trade quality for speed. Features turned off "way too aggressively on computers", and anything that turns itself off should say so and offer a force-on switch.
Taste from pictures: "For the neon I was hoping for a look like this…" Unity had "way less character… should feel way less sterile."
True to real places, but no brands, team names or logos, and wording that shows a place rather than claims it's accurate.

The pattern: the suite is strong at one object at a time. Terrarium's hard problems were at the scale of a whole town, a running game, and a real phone.

Biggest time-savers, ranked

  1. Look lab. Compare whole-scene looks side by side against your reference pictures, with saturation and contrast checks, applied the same way to the game, baked assets, thumbnails and videos.157 look commits, 57 fixes; the first-visit look changed four times.
  2. Proof on real devices. Lock time, weather, look and device; render fixed views; diff pictures; measure memory and frame time on a real iPhone; brand and wording checks in the same gate.123 phone/speed commits; check_release.mjs, ab_fps.mjs, speed_views.mjs built by hand.
  3. Real places to scenes. Area in; LiDAR, OpenStreetMap, footprints and aerial photos out as terrain, buildings, roads and water. Hand fixes stored as layers that survive a rebuild.20+ fetch_*.py, seven fixer scripts, hand-fixes.md.
  4. Life, crowds and traffic. Who is where, doing what, by hour, season and event; steering that never overlaps; vehicle rigs and lanes.~12k lines across 11 life/wild modules; taxi.js, traffic.js; walker collision only at D183.
  5. Overlap and grounding checks for moving things. Extend the studio's scene checks to a running game.~55 "through" or "inside" commits.
  6. Capture and trailer maker. Shot list, fixed-step frames from the game itself, a cut to the beat with captions, every aspect ratio.Five recuts; ?capture=1, watchshots.js, 576 MB of video by hand.
  7. Environment presets. Real sun and stars, weather history or live, tides, water and puddles.sky.js, dayweather.js, tides.js, water.js (2,130 lines).
  8. One model, offline and at runtime. Let the browser build variants from the same op graph instead of copying measurements between JS and Blender.claysculpt_dump.mjs, kit.py "same measurements the viewer uses".
