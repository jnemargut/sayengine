How Characters Get Their Shape

Architecture 🚪 One-way door High stakes

This is your sculpting question. Characters are the hardest asset: they have to look right, bend at the joints without tearing, and carry a skeleton that both Godot and three.js understand (decision 13). How the shape gets made decides almost everything about rigging and animation (decision 16). Your choices so far point one way: low-poly with baked materials (12), everything checkable by Claude, and an import feature being built right now that can bring in outside meshes and reference images.

Parametric Character Kits

Illustration. One humanoid template, three sets of settings.
# what you'd type /studio make a stocky blacksmith, short, big arms, leather apron
Pipeline per character Pick templateSet proportionsAdd parts (hair, apron, tools)Skeleton + weights come with itPose checks Have it / built once · AI does well · AI struggles
Characters are built like the stool: a template body (humanoid first, then a four-legged one) whose height, build and head size are settings, plus parts like hair, clothes and tools added as ops. Joint edge loops, skeleton and skin weights are designed once per template, so every character Claude makes is born rigged, bends cleanly, and uses bone names Godot and three.js retarget automatically. The import feature adds a hybrid: bring in an AI-generated or sketched reference, and Claude fits the kit's settings to match it.

Works well when

  • Low-poly game characters, many of them
  • Every character must animate reliably
  • "Make him taller" stays one setting

Watch out for

  • Characters can look samey without strong parts
  • Each new body type is a template to build

Sculpt Mode in the Studio

Illustration. Freeform shapes from blended blobs (signed-distance shapes).
Pipeline per character Blend shapesSculpt detailLow-poly retopologyPlace skeletonPaint weights
Add a sculpting mode: blend smooth shapes together and push and pull the surface. Manifold, the studio's geometry engine, can already turn blended-shape descriptions into meshes, so creatures and blobby forms are within reach. But a sculpt gives a dense mesh with no joint loops, so every character still needs retopology, skeleton placement and weight painting, the steps where AI output most needs a person. Brush-based sculpting is also a human activity; Claude can't push a brush well.

Works well when

  • Organic creatures and one-off hero shapes
  • You enjoy sculpting by hand

Watch out for

  • Rigging becomes manual for every character
  • Off-style for low-poly; big new tool to build

AI-Generated, Then Cleaned Up

Illustration. A generator's dense mesh, then automated cleanup.
Pipeline per character Prompt / imageImportRetopology to low-polyAuto-rigFix weights
Type a prompt or give an image to a generator like Meshy or Tripo, then import the result and clean it up automatically. It gives the most variety the fastest, and the import feature being built covers the first step. But generators produce hundreds of thousands of triangles with no edge flow; auto-riggers misplace bones on stylized proportions; and you depend on a paid service. Some open models (Hunyuan3D) aren't licensed for use in the EU or UK.

Works well when

  • You need lots of varied characters quickly
  • Background characters that barely animate

Watch out for

  • Cleanup and rigging fail on stylized shapes
  • Paid service dependency; licence limits

Hand Off to Blender

Illustration. Characters made in Blender, props in the studio.
Pipeline per character Claude scripts BlenderSculpt / modelAuto weightsFix by handImport back
Characters get made in Blender, which has every tool, and Claude drives it through Blender MCP (the biggest agent-for-3D project, 33k stars). The studio imports the finished character for checks and export. It needs no new studio tools, but it's the "AI uses Blender" model you set out to replace: no settings-driven characters, no branching review, and Blender's own learning curve when something goes wrong.

Works well when

  • One or two hero characters with special needs
  • You already know Blender

Watch out for

  • Leaves the AI-first studio for the hardest assets
  • Review and undo happen outside your history

The case against my pick

Kits are how you get a hundred reliable characters, and also how you get a hundred characters that look related. Templates take real up-front work: a humanoid template with clean joint loops and good weights is days of careful design, and every new body type (a bird, a snake, a robot) is another one. A designer who wants one memorable, odd-shaped hero will hit the kit's limits. If varied, surprising characters matter more than reliable animation, a hybrid of C for shapes plus A's skeleton is worth considering, accepting that cleanup sometimes fails.

This recommendation assumes

  • Low-poly characters (decision 12) with simple, readable shapes. Kits fit this best.
  • Animation reliability matters more than one-off shape variety: the rig comes free with every kit character.
  • Templates use standard humanoid bone names and a T-pose, so Godot's retargeter and three.js's retarget helpers map them with no manual setup (decision 16).
  • The import feature becomes a reference path: an AI-generated or sketched shape guides the kit's settings, without being used as the final mesh.
  • Blender (D) stays an escape hatch for a rare hero character, not the pipeline.

What the research says

  • MakeHuman/MPFB's base mesh, shape targets and proxies are CC0, so characters made from them can ship in closed games. That makes "a kit with known topology and a known skeleton" legally clean to build on or learn from. MPFB FAQ
  • Manifold, the studio's geometry engine, can turn blended-shape (signed-distance) descriptions into meshes. Blobby bodies or a basic sculpt mode need no new library. manifold-3d
  • AI generators output hundreds of thousands of triangles with no edge flow; most sources say retopology and rigging still need a person. Vendor low-poly modes claim big reductions, unverified. gitconnected
  • Meshy's auto-rig misplaces bones if the model isn't in a T- or A-pose and deforms badly on proportions far from human, a risk for stylized characters. Meshy docs
  • Hunyuan3D's community licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea. Hunyuan3D licence
  • Blender MCP is the largest agent-for-3D project (about 33k stars). Community servers expose automatic weights. blender-mcp Glama

Where experts disagree

Generative-AI vendors say prompt-to-character with auto-rigging is ready for games. Technical artists say generated meshes still need retopology and weight fixes before they animate well, especially for stylized proportions. For low-poly characters you can describe with settings, kits sidestep the argument. For characters you can't, the vendors' tools are the faster start but the riskier finish.
