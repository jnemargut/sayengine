Each proposal arrives as a short list of meaningful changes — "back angle 14→18°", "back curve +30 mm", "arm rests added" — with its check results (step 1). Hover a change and the affected part glows sage in the viewport; hold Space to see before (step 2). Keep or undo each change individually (step 3), then commit only what you kept (step 4). Works well when

  • Some changes depend on each other (undo one breaks another)
  • Raw mesh edits don't summarize into neat lines

The case against my pick

Change lists turn a visual craft into reading a diff — the moment of "ooh, that's nicer" happens in the viewport, not a list, and a ghost overlay (A) or card flicking (B) delivers that faster. Per-change keep/undo also gets hairy when changes depend on each other (undoing "back angle" may invalidate "arm rests" that were attached to the old angle), so you'll need dependency rules and re-running checks after every partial keep. If most real requests produce one cohesive change, the granularity is overhead.

This recommendation assumes

  • Changes are grouped by the AI into named, meaningful steps (one ~ one typed op or parameter set), not raw patches
  • When there are several alternatives, B's swap cards sit above the change list as tabs (p1 · p2 · p3)
  • Dependent changes are linked — undoing a parent prompts about its children; checks re-run after any partial keep
  • Nothing is deleted: undone changes and discarded proposals stay as archived branches
  • Tripwire: if testers accept or discard whole proposals >90% of the time, collapse the list behind a "details" toggle

What the research says

  • Cursor users strongly defend per-change Keep/Undo review and push back when it's replaced by whole-session accept. Cursor forum
  • Figma Make keeps AI edits uncommitted until Apply, yet users report losing large amounts of work — proposal/commit separation must be airtight. Figma forum
  • Onshape surfaces conflicting merges as red errors to repair rather than blocking — a model for dependent-change conflicts. Onshape merging
