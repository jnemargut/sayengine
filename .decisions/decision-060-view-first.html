Which Systems Join the Live View First

Information architecture 🔁 Two-way door High stakes

Modelling works so well together because we both look at one live view (localhost:4517), you click a part to say "this", and every change I make is a step you keep or undo. Everything else lives somewhere else. Motion, creatures, cast, cutscenes and voice lines each have their own separate player page, effects and UI are still pictures, sound is numbers, and town life has no view at all. Bringing all of them into the live view at once is too much, so this picks which group goes first, while your own game is the next thing you build.

The case against my pick

Decision 59 taught us that tools pulled by a real game beat tools built ahead of one, and A builds ahead. If your game turns out to be a calm town builder or a puzzle game with little animation, the moving-things timeline is the wrong first investment, and B would have found that out for free. It's a lean, not a strong pick, because nearly every game has characters, effects and sound, but "nearly" is a bet.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • The live view stays the one place we both look at (localhost:4517). The five separate player pages retire once their system is in.
  • Whatever goes first, the pieces are built so later systems plug in the same way (a light version of D's frame, grown from a real system rather than designed up front).
  • Tripwire: if your game's spec (from /x-decide-gaming) uses none of the chosen group, switch to B for the rest.
  • Tripwire: if the first group takes more than about two weeks, ship what's working and start your game anyway.
