How One-Off Motions Get Made From Words

Technical 🚪 One-way door High stakes

Rig parts (decision 52) handle ongoing motion: walking, swimming, flapping, rolling, swaying. But many things you'll ask for are one-off actions: "the octopus pours a cup of tea", "the windmill creaks to a stop", "the dog jumps onto the bench and spins". This decides how those get made. Research: AI text-to-motion models are humanoid-only, most can't be used commercially (their training data bans it), and the good ones need a 24 GB NVIDIA GPU. Video capture works on a Mac but only for human motion.

Claude writes a few key moments as goals for each part (where tentacle tips go, where the body leans, when), and the studio solves the in-between with IK, easing and follow-through from springs. It checks contacts and limits, and you review the clip like any change. "Pour slower, and more nervously" adjusts timing and style.
Claude acts as the animator: it writes a short list of key moments for the parts involved (where hands, feet, tentacle tips or the body go, and when), the way recent research uses language models to plan just the end points and lets a solver do the rest. The studio fills in the motion with IK, timing curves and follow-through from spring parts, runs the checks (decision 54), and makes it a reviewable clip. It works on any rig built from parts, runs locally and is yours to ship. Your notes ("slower", "more nervous", "bigger anticipation") edit it. For human actions, you can optionally film yourself as a starting point (local, free).

The case against my pick

Key-posed motion made by a language model is only as good as its sense of timing and weight, and subtle human acting (a hesitant reach, a shrug) is where A will look most mechanical; an AI motion model would do human actions more naturally today, if you accepted the licence and hardware limits. Complex actions may take several rounds of notes, and each round is a render to watch. The research behind A (planning just the end points) is recent and mostly shown on humans.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes the rig parts from decision 52 expose goals Claude can set (tip positions, body lean, hinge angles) and the studio solves the rest.
  • Assumes webcam capture (MediaPipe, local) is an optional starting point for human actions, not the main path.
  • Tripwire: if a commercially safe any-skeleton motion model appears that runs on your Mac, add it as a draft generator inside A.
  • Tripwire: if common human actions keep taking three or more rounds of notes, add a small free clip library (D) as starting points.
