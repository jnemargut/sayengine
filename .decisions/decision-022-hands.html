Decision 22 of 25 · Faces, hands and cutscenes

Hands

Architecture 🚪 One-way door Medium stakes

In cutscenes, hands do a lot of the acting: pointing, gripping a hammer, a fist on the table, an open palm while explaining. This decision sets how much hand the kit template carries (finger bones), how a pose is described (so "loosen his grip" is a setting), and how hands hold props without fingers sinking into them. It's built into the kit template, so it's decided now, before week 3.

Full Fingers, Poses as Settings, Grips on Props

Illustration. Low-poly fingers with 3 joints each; the hammer brings its own grip.
# what Claude does studio set-hand --character blacksmith --side right --pose '{"curl":0.7,"spread":0.1,"thumb":0.6}' studio hold --character blacksmith --hand right --prop hammer
The kit hand has five fingers with three joints each. A hand pose is a handful of numbers: curl per finger, spread and thumb. So "loosen his grip" is one setting, and named poses (relaxed, fist, point, pinch, open palm) are just saved settings. Props carry their own grip socket: the hammer handle knows where the palm goes and what the fingers do, so "hold the hammer" snaps cleanly with no fingers sinking into wood. A check catches any finger passing through a held prop.

Works well when

  • Hands act in close-up cutscenes
  • Characters hold tools and props
  • Gestures come from the performance layer (23)

Watch out for

  • Each prop needs a grip socket defined
  • 15 more bones per hand, per character

Mitten Hands (Thumb, Index, Rest)

Illustration. Three moving parts per hand.
~6 bonesper hand
Classic low-polymany indie games
Limited gesturesno counting, no pinch
A simplified hand: thumb, index finger, and the other three fingers moving as one block. It's very common in low-poly games, light, and easy to pose; pointing and gripping still work. But close-up cutscene gestures suffer: no pinch, no counting on fingers, no nervous finger tapping. A whole category of hand acting is unavailable.

Works well when

  • Hands are rarely in close-up
  • Very low-poly, chunky style

Watch out for

  • Limits hand acting in cutscenes
  • Upgrading later changes every character

Full Fingers + Procedural Grasping

Illustration. Fingers solve against the prop's surface at runtime.
Any propno setup per prop
Per-finger tuningto avoid clipping
Engine work ×2Godot + three.js
Same fingers as A, but grips are solved automatically: fingers curl until they touch the prop's surface. Any object can be picked up with no setup per prop. But research and commercial VR tools show it's fiddly: fingers clip through surfaces, need per-finger tuning, and grips look subtly wrong. Commercial tools fall back to fixed, authored grab poses. It's also engine-side code to write twice.

Works well when

  • Players pick up arbitrary objects (VR, physics games)

Watch out for

  • Clipping and odd-looking grips
  • Engine code in both targets

Swappable Hand Shapes, No Finger Bones

Illustration. Whole hand shapes swapped in, like a toy figure.
0 finger boneslightest
4–8 posesper character
Popsno in-between
No finger bones: each hand has a handful of fixed shapes (open, fist, point, grip) that swap whole. It's the lightest option and fits a very toy-like style. But hands pop from shape to shape with nothing in between, which looks stiff in close-up cutscenes, and there's no "loosen the grip a little". Natural hand acting, which you asked for, isn't possible.

Works well when

  • Toy or figurine style
  • Hands never in close-up

Watch out for

  • Stiff, popping hands
  • No natural hand acting

The case against my pick

Fifteen finger joints per hand roughly doubles each character's bone count (from about 25 to about 55). Web and mobile budgets (decision 17) will feel it, and the weight painting around every knuckle is where the kit template's skin weights get hardest. Grip sockets also mean every prop a hand touches needs one defined. That's easy for the studio's own props, but imported meshes need a manual step. If your cutscenes are mostly medium shots where hands rarely fill the screen, B's mitten hands cover 80% of the acting at a fraction of the cost.

This recommendation assumes

  • Hands appear in close-up in cutscenes, so gestures need real fingers.
  • The kit template designs hand topology and knuckle weights once; every character inherits them.
  • Studio props get a generated grip socket from their shape (handle = power grip, small object = pinch), which Claude can adjust; imported props get one placed by hand.
  • Game profiles may drop finger bones for distant LODs or web-mobile targets, keeping them for cutscenes.

What the research says

  • Fingers passing through held objects hurts grasp and release, and avoiding it needs per-finger calibration. Commercial hand tools fall back to fixed, authored grab poses per object. Prachyabrued & Borst, 3DUI 2012 Auto Hand docs
  • Godot's humanoid skeleton profile and its retargeter include full finger bones with standard names, so kit hands following them retarget hand animations automatically. Godot docs
  • Learned gesture models generate body and hand motion, but their gestures fit the speech only slightly better than chance, and their outputs carry non-commercial licences. Hand gestures are better as chosen poses (decision 23). GENEA 2023 SMPL-X licence
