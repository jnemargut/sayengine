The Hand-off From Decisions to Building

Technical 🚪 One-way door High stakes

"A planning layer that's easy to execute on." On the toy-first road (decision 37), building happens after every few decisions, not once at the end, so the hand-off runs many times per game. What it feels like: each build runs as studio proposals you can keep or undo. When it hits a question none of your decisions answer ("what happens when the cart tips over?"), it stops and shows a small decision page instead of guessing. Change decision 3 later, and only the parts that came from decision 3 rebuild.
Decisions compile into a typed game spec (.decisions/game-spec.json), where every field cites the decision it came from. /x-game-build turns the spec into a step list of studio, /game and module calls, and runs each step as a studio proposal with its checks. When a step needs a choice no decision covers, it stops and makes a small decision page (decision-kit's "state your case" pattern) instead of deciding silently. Change a decision, and only the steps that cite it rebuild.

Works well when

  • Building happens many times per game
  • You want no silent decisions during building
  • Changing one decision shouldn't redo everything

Watch out for

  • The spec format has to grow with the tools
  • Stops can feel frequent early on

The case against my pick

A typed spec is one more format to design and keep in step with every tool, and the tools are still growing weekly (weeks 5–14). Early on it will be missing fields, so builds will stop often, and a stream of mini decision pages about cart tipping and door hinges is the decision fatigue decision-kit warns about. B works today with nothing new, and Claude Code with good skills makes sensible small calls; you could add the spec later once you see which gaps actually matter.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes small, low-stakes gaps can be filled by a default and listed afterwards, with stops reserved for gaps that change how the game plays or looks.
  • Assumes every build step runs as a studio proposal, so anything can be kept or undone.
  • Tripwire: if a build stops more than three times for one toy, raise the threshold for what counts as a real gap.
  • Tripwire: if the spec needs a new field every week, keep it loose (named settings) until the tools settle.
