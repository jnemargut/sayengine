Shape of the Game Toolset

Architecture 🚪 One-way door High stakes

Today you have one thing: a studio where Claude builds and checks 3D objects while you review. Games also need characters that move, levels, game logic, and something that actually runs the game and lets the AI play it. The question is where those live. Does this one studio grow into all of it? Does it split into a family of studios? Does it stay the place assets are made, while a real game engine runs the game? Or do you build an AI-first engine? Everything after this (art style, engine, characters, rigging) hangs off this answer.

The case against my pick

Option C splits the world in two, and the seam is exactly where game pipelines hurt most. Godot drops material edits on reimport, Unity's glTF importer only plays animations through its legacy system, and skinned characters are the flakiest part of every engine's import. Worse, anything you or Claude change inside the engine (a level layout, a script) doesn't get the studio's branching history or keep-or-undo review, which is the thing that makes this studio special. If what you love is "every change is reviewable", Option B keeps that everywhere, at the cost of owning a small runtime.

This recommendation assumes

  • You want to make games, not sell an engine. If the engine is the product, revisit D.
  • The studio's edge is AI-made, checked, reviewable assets, and no engine offers that today.
  • An engine can be driven well enough from the command line for Claude to run and screenshot games (confirmed for Godot; decision 13 picks).
  • The hand-off is owned by a checked export contract with round-trip tests, not hoped for.

What the research says

  • Blender built a game engine into its modeler, then deleted it in 2.8. Autodesk paired Maya LT with its Stingray engine and killed the engine in 2018, citing customers standardizing on Unity and Unreal. In both cases the modeler survived and the engine didn't. 80.lv CG Channel
  • All three big engines and Roblox now let AI agents drive their editors. Unreal has official MCP support in 5.8 (experimental) and Unity in its AI Assistant (beta), and Roblox Studio has a built-in MCP server plus a playtesting agent. 44% of Roblox's top 1,000 creators already used AI tools. Epic Unity Roblox
  • None of those engine integrations does parametric, branching, reviewable asset making with checks. The gap is upstream of the engines, which is where this studio already sits. Meshy and Blender MCP also win by feeding engines, not replacing them. Meshy blender-mcp
  • On a benchmark of 132 Godot game tasks, the best AI agent passed only 53.8%, and it did worst on 3D graphics (38%) and animation (33%). Letting the agent see screenshots of the running game raised one model from 41% to 52%. Whatever you pick, Claude must be able to run and look at the game. GameDevBench, arXiv
  • The "AI-native engines" of 2026 (nAIVE, Euca, Egos and others) are all early, with no shipped games. Rosebud, an all-in-one AI game maker, hits walls on export and trust. Cinevva Ludo
  • Solo developers who set out to make a game regularly end up with "a short proof-of-concept demo" and an engine. Long-lived solo engines run 90k–120k lines over 7–14 years. Game Developer nCine
  • Pipelines break at the hand-off. Godot loses built-in materials on reimport, Unity's glTF importer can strip normal maps without warning, and skinned-mesh import is the weakest spot in Unreal. Godot forums Unity glTFast

Where experts disagree

Engine pragmatists say never build your own unless the game's design is truly unique. The AI-native camp says today's engines were built for clicking humans, and that an agent-first engine is the real long-term play, as Roblox's move to agents hints. What decides it for you: do you want to ship games in the next year (C), or is the engine itself the thing you'd sell (D)?
