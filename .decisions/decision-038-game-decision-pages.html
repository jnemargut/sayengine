What a Game Decision Page Shows

Visual 🔁 Two-way door High stakes

Decision-kit's rule is "show, don't tell": a UI choice gets four working mockups, a schema gets four sets of tables. For games, most early choices are about feel (camera, controls, how the cart handles), and you can't judge feel from a picture. This decides what the four options on a game decision page actually are, given the road you chose (decision 37: toy first).

The case against my pick

Playable options are slow to make: each feel decision needs a working toy before the page can open, so the pause between decisions grows from seconds to minutes, and a broken toy blocks the decision. Four kinds of media means four ways a page can fail. C's clips are a reasonable middle: motion is visible, pages open instantly, and you can review them on your phone, with the playable toy saved for the one or two decisions where hands-on feel really matters.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes the toy is parametric, so four options are four settings on one web build, not four builds.
  • Assumes playable options are limited to feel decisions (about 3–5 per game); everything else uses renders, clips or diagrams.
  • Tripwire: if a playable page takes more than about 5 minutes to appear, fall back to clips for that decision.
  • Without the studio installed (decision 36), pages fall back to D's diagrams.
