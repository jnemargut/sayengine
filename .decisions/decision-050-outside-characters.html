Outside Characters as a Side Door

Technical 🔁 Two-way door Medium stakes

Most characters will come from the studio (decisions 47–49), but now and then you may want a model from VRoid, an asset store, a free pack or Blender. Today any of those arrives as a statue. This decides how far the side door opens: which outside characters can walk, talk and appear in cutscenes, and what happens to the ones that can't.

What it feels like: drop in a VRoid knight and the report says what carried over. It walks and takes part in cutscenes, its own vowel shapes become most of the 15 speech shapes, and its licence says personal use only. A model with no skeleton comes in as a prop, with a note on how to rig it.
Characters that already have a skeleton (glTF, GLB, VRM) come in with their bones matched automatically to the standard humanoid names, so studio motion, life layer and cutscenes play on them (retargeted by our own code, since the built-in three.js version is unreliable). Faces use what the model brings: ARKit-style shapes first, then VRM's five vowels with consonants made from them, then a jaw if it has one, otherwise eyes only. The import reads the licence stored in VRM files and warns before you ship. Models without a skeleton stay props.

Works well when

  • VRoid, free packs (KayKit is CC0 and rigged), store characters
  • You want to know what carried over
  • Licences checked for you

Watch out for

  • Unrigged models stay props
  • Faces vary with what the model brings

What it feels like: even a statue can walk. But only if it stands in a T or A pose with arms apart, and long hair, skirts and capes come out wrong.
Everything in A, plus a local skeleton-adder for models that have none: render the model, find joints from the front and side, place bones and weights. On a Mac this has to be our own simpler method, because every machine-learning auto-rigger needs an NVIDIA GPU and Mixamo is online-only and in maintenance mode. It works on clean T- or A-pose humanoids and struggles with hair, skirts, capes and fused arms.

Works well when

Watch out for

  • Unreliable on clothing and hair
  • A sizeable build for a side door

The case against my pick

You said characters should mostly come from the studio, so even A's importer may rarely be used, and D would put that effort into studio characters instead. Bone matching and retargeting are fiddly (rest poses, A- versus T-pose, missing finger bones), and faces will be inconsistent across imports. C's tested shelf might cover the "I need a stand-in" case with a fraction of the work.

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Assumes the studio's skeleton uses VRM/Godot humanoid names, so most rigged models map automatically.
  • Assumes our own retargeter (rest-pose correction plus hip-height scaling), since three.js's built-in one is unreliable.
  • Tripwire: if you import unrigged models more than a couple of times, add B's skeleton-adder.
