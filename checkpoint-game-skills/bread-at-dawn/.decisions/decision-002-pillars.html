Three Pillars

Concept 🔁 Two-way door High stakes

A pillar is a short statement every feature in Bread at Dawn has to pass through; three of them decide what gets built and what gets cut. Each set below shows, for every pillar, one feature it lets in (✓) and one it keeps out (✕).

Option A
My pick: fits the fantasyLean

Cozy · Hills · Waking Town

Cozy, never rushedA soft sunrise glow as time passesA fail screen when you're late
Hills are the puzzleChoosing the long flat road or the steep shortcutA map that marks the best route for you
The town wakes as you goWindows light up behind youCustomers who never appear
What it feels like: every feature asks: is it calm, is it about the hills, does the town wake?
Keeps the pace gentle, makes geography the challenge and rewards you with a town that changes. Every later feature has to be calm, about routes, or about the town waking. It fits the chosen fantasy line almost word for word.

Works well when

  • The fantasy is the dawn courier
  • You want a clear filter for cutting

Watch out for

  • "Hills" must stay fun, not tiring

Option B

Warmth · People · Routes

Warm bread, cold morningSteam fading as loaves coolA score for speed
Every house is a personThe fisherman always wants ryeIdentical doorsteps
Tiny town, big routesBack alleys that link upA second town
What it feels like: every feature asks: is it warm, is it a person, does it link the town?
Puts warmth and the townspeople first. Good for a character-rich game, but "warm bread, cold morning" quietly invites a timer, and the people pillar means writing many small characters.

Works well when

  • Characters matter most

Watch out for

  • Timer creep
  • Lots of characters

Values over mechanics: small, kind, lit by dawn. Very clear about tone and what to cut (no coins, no upgrades), less clear about what the challenge is.

Respond with: "Option D"

The case against my pick

This is a lean, not a strong pick: C's "pushing feels good" pillar is the one that protects the toy, and A has nothing that says the cart itself must feel great. If the toy at step 2 is the make-or-break, swap "The town wakes as you go" for "Pushing feels good".

Assumptions and tripwires

  • Pillars can be rewritten freely until the loop is decided (step 4).
  • Tripwire: if a playtest finding fits no pillar, the pillars are missing something.
