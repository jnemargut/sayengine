# Design decision catalogue

How to spread the four options for each design decision, and what each card shows.

## Camera + controls + character (step 3)

Combine them; don't split. Spread across distance and agency:

- close follow behind the character (intimate, harder to plan routes),
- high follow or three-quarter top-down (readable, good for routes and puzzles),
- fixed or room camera (cinematic, simple controls),
- free orbit (exploration, more controls to learn).

Each option names the controls in plain words (WASD to push, Space to brake) and the character's body plan if the studio has a character for it. Compare: readability of the space · how the verb feels · phone friendliness · motion sickness risk · undo cost.

Visual: playable presets of the current toy (iframe per card, `?preset=A`…). Try each in the toy's real space first. A camera that needs room the toy doesn't have (a high follow over small rooms with no ceiling) is fixed, swapped, or marked "untestable here" on its card. If the user named a camera up front (`game.stated`), one card is that camera.

## Feel (step 3)

Name the feel in human words, then the numbers behind it. Typical spread for a moving verb: heavy, floaty, snappy, drifty. For a throwing verb: lobbed, flat, spinning, magnetic. Mention the juice each preset carries (dust puffs, a small screen shake, squash).

Compare: how it feels in the first ten seconds · how it feels after five minutes · skill ceiling · fits the pillars · undo cost.

Visual: playable presets.

Systemic games: one measured line per card on how the preset meets the other characters' senses (who hears a landing, from how far). If no preset changes what the system notices, say that too.

## Juice (optional, step 3)

Four bundles of small reactions in order of impact: sound + squash; dust + shake; slow-motion on success; particles and screen flash. Visual: 4-second clips or presets.

## The loop (step 4)

Loop-led: four loops of three to five steps, each with the verb at the centre, a lap time, and what grows each lap. Toy-led: four reasons to come back (collect, decorate, discover, share). Story-led: four opening scenes.

Compare: lap time · what grows · fits the pillars · build effort for a grey box · undo cost.

Visual: a loop diagram (circle of steps, verb in the middle) per option.

## Asset sources (step 6, first)

Four mixes of free sources, for props, the world, the hero and the people:

- all studio-built (one consistent style, lowest fidelity ceiling),
- CC0 library props and textures (Poly Haven, ambientCG, Kenney, Quaternius) plus studio characters,
- Blender-built hero or props, headless and scripted, plus CC0 props,
- a local generator for props, cleaned up in the studio or Blender.

Each card: the same prop and the hero rendered from that source, licence line, what it costs to keep consistent, and whether the studio can still check and export it. Never a paid option. Compare: matches the fidelity target · consistency · hero risk · build time · undo cost.

## The look (step 6)

Four looks that change mood, not just hue: for example warm toy, crisp daylight, dusk with long shadows, flat poster. Each card is a render of the actual scene in that look, with a palette strip and, when the look lab exists, its colourfulness and palette numbers.

Compare: readability of the verb · mood · fits the pillars · phone cost · undo cost.

## World (step 6)

Real, invented, remix, or a single handcrafted room. Size in streets, rooms or minutes. Visual: top-down map renders or sketches.

## Set dressing (step 6)

How lived-in the place is, and what the things in it say about who lives there. Spread: cozy clutter · genuinely messy · neat and sparse · changes through play (tidy at the start, wrecked at the end). A second page, when the cast is known, can cover **what a character's stuff says about them** (a hobby, a job, a passion), with the props that become gameplay named. If the user has said a word ("messy"), one card is that word taken literally. Visual: one room or street dressed each way. Compare: readability of what you can interact with · what it says about the characters · prop count and build time · undo cost.

## Cast (step 6)

Four casting options, each a small set of characters (body plan, face style, role). Visual: the studio's cast lineup render; flag look-alikes the cast check would catch. Render each at the fidelity target, not as grey kit defaults; a cast that looks like toys when the target is realistic misleads the choice.

## Tone, title screen and logo (step 6, when needed)

Tone: four levels, including one that escalates during play; each shown as a sample job list, one character line and a title card. Title screen: four short openings that show the fantasy in under six seconds and can be skipped with any key; visual: a still or a clip each, with the working title in it.

## Sound and music direction (optional, step 6)

Four directions described in a sentence each plus the instruments or textures; clips from the studio's sound module when present. Respect the user's local-and-free rule for any generated audio.
